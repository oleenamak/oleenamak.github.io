American Operator acquires small businesses through an ownership model designed to keep them locally owned and operated. $50M Series B, led by 8VC and Human Capital.

I led the growth function through a business model pivot, helping American Operator build a repeatable way to find qualified buyers and sellers and turn that demand into a pipeline of small-business acquisitions. Channels I owned included: paid acquisition, lifecycle marketing and CRM, PR, social, and content. Explore some of my favorite moments below.

When I joined Mainshares, growth depended on supply: more acquisition deals on the platform for investors to back. Buyers can spend years searching before they acquire a business, so we decided to engage them much earlier in that journey. Operator Network, a community we launched on Circle, was built to do that. It attracted aspiring buyers early, kept them engaged through a long search, and moved them to Mainshares once they were ready to raise capital.

I led the launch, starting with positioning. Through rapid testing across channels and messaging, I worked out what buyers valued, where we stood apart from competitors, and what turned interest into sign-ups. With that in place, I owned lifecycle and CRM and designed the buyer journey end to end, from acquisition and onboarding through nurture to the handoff to Sales.

Within a quarter, Operator Network grew to 6,000+ members. We ran 25+ webinars, workshops and office hours with outside experts and internal specialists, and the network drove two deals into the opportunity pipeline, an early sign that the community could produce deal flow.

### Scaling Locally Owned and Operated

The certification program relied on manual outreach and fulfillment, and applications sat in Airtable outside the Deal team’s CRM. I tested Facebook ads as a way to reach more independent business owners and brought in a fulfillment partner to mail certification stickers in batches as application volume grew.

I worked with the Deal team to capture details like industry and expected sale timeline in HubSpot, then launched a Facebook group to give certified owners a reason to stay connected. The program grew to 6,000+ certified businesses, with Facebook generating qualified leads at $4–$5 each and acquisition and fulfillment costing about $10 per certified business.

### Securing national media coverage

When Mainshares became American Operator, we had to explain an unfamiliar acquisition model to owners weighing what would happen to their business after they sold. I worked with the CEO and Brand team on the relaunch messaging and helped develop our first case study to show how the model worked in practice.

I ran PR in-house and secured coverage in Bloomberg, Fox Business, the New York Post, Inc., and Entrepreneur. Across the broader relaunch, inbound leads grew 15× in one quarter and accounted for about 24% of total lead acquisition.
